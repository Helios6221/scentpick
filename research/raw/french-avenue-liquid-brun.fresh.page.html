From delicious to straight up dirt
Disclaimer: I am a trained geologist, I've smelled and tasted dirt.

Disclaimer 2: I practice downhill skateboarding, I eat dirt more often than I would like to admit and probably way more often that you do.

Following the disclaimers, I confess that I totally fell for the hype when buying this fragrance. I was just finding out about fragrantica, parfumo and the hellhole of FragTube and found a good deal for LB so I went forward.

To my nose the opening was a little too agressive, but only for the very few instants of first exposure to the air. The typical alcool odour of perfumes but slightly more metallic.

Tried it on paper and tissue the first time since I was already wearing something else and found the smell pleasing. I had never smelled a "gourmand" fragrance, but it instantly made sense. The smell is sweet and indeed very reminiscing of praliné chocolate with a floral twist that remains "masculine". Mouthwatering. No comments on the dry down on paper. Impressive longevity on clothes.

Tried it on my skin and there I knew that I really really enjoyed the smell and how it smelled on me. I liked that it is strong, pungent, chocolatey, yet very airy and slightly floral. I wanted to be smelled during a hug. I wanted my neck bitten.

Now, for the dirty unexpected twist:
This might be something caused by the interaction between the fragrance and my skin/sweat since I still have not yet come across anything like that which I am about to type.

Against all recommendations, I decided to try this fragrance on a HOT day, not only that, but the occasion was a skateboarding session in a street spot. I have a few observations to share.

1) If your body heats up, a fragrance that already has beastly projection becomes truly satanic. I felt like I was radiating this out-of-place delicious smell way more so than I usually would be comfortable doing. Like dressing a little too extravagant for a skating session, I felt like a was screaming to be perceived. Like dressing a little too extravagant for a skating session, you better deliver a great show if you're gonna pull that much attention.

2) After some hours of skating intensely, sweating heavily, soaked in my own sweat to be precise, I got a strong smell of dirt. The kind of smell you get after being in a dust storm or after a day of playing rugby or any other rough sport on a dry field. But weirdly enough, I did not fall that day... No part of my body had touched the ground that day besides a hand, maybe, at some point.
No sweetness, no praliné, just dirt and maybe the faintest hint of musk to give the smell of dirt on skin. No joke, construction worker forearm smell.

Sweat and dirt, what? The sweat was mine, but earth was absolutely not what I was expecting even though I had been explicitly looking elsewhere for something that reminded me of soil. This was the driest soil, blown by hot winds landing on my sweaty skin. I really like this. I really really like this, but I am not sure if as a follow-up to gourmand. Going from edible to dirt sounds like catfishing to me, but I am definitely going to try to pull that out again by late fall and winter.

To double down on the "my sweat"/LB hypothesis, I tried LB before going to sleep on a hot night and, surprise! Woke up smelling like dirt! If someone had told me that I had rolled myself in a gravel pit while sleep walking and came back to bed I would believed the statement purely based on the smell. Now I am not sure if I'll wear this in an erotic context, even though I personally enjoy the smell of dirt.

Bought LB LE afterwards since I found a great deal on it. I am looking forward to try these once the weather cools down.

Is Liquid Brun suitable for a clinic?
Is Liquid Brun suitable for a clinic?

yes — but with conditions.

This is a warm, sweet vanilla fragrance with a bit of weight, so it is not the safest “office/clinic scent” if oversprayed.

However, in a cold, well air-conditioned clinic, it can work nicely and feel very polished, especially during morning or evening shifts.

The key is moderation: few sprays at most, avoid spraying close to patients, and do not reapply inside the consultation room.

In a clinic, a fragrance should say:
“I’m well-groomed,”
not:
“I arrived five minutes before I did.”

From Curiosity to Discomfort
I really wanted to like this one especially considering the massive hype around it and even around the OG it’s inspired by. But after testing it properly, I have to be honest: I just don’t understand the hype at all.

From the very first spray, the scent hit me in a way that was immediately uncomfortable. Instead of feeling warm, smooth, or inviting, it gave me a straight headache. There’s something in the composition that comes across as sharp, heavy, and overwhelming to my nose. Rather than developing nicely on the skin, it stays unpleasant and tiring throughout the wear.

Looking at the notes, on paper it sounds great: orange blossom, cardamom, cinnamon, bourbon vanilla, praline, woods and musk. In reality, though, the spices feel overly aggressive and the sweetness comes across as cloying rather than creamy. The vanilla and praline, which should be cozy and comforting, feel synthetic and dense, almost suffocating. The woody base and ambroxan don’t smooth things out for me instead, they amplify the heaviness.

I can see why some people might enjoy this DNA, especially if you like bold, sweet-spicy gourmand scents. But for my personal taste and my skin chemistry, this one is a hard no. It’s not just not for me it’s actively unpleasant and gives me discomfort when wearing it.

Hype aside, this is a reminder that popularity doesn’t equal personal enjoyment. Always test before buying.
